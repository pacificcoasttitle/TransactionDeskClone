Independent Peer Review

In a rapidly changing world, information and communication technology has entered a new era of speed. Information can be shared quickly and freely online through electronic journals, databases, and websites. This does allow scholars to share information quickly and resourcefully. Although this is a huge advantage for scholars, it does bring a completely new set of problems.

One serious obstacle blocks this instant sharing of information. Even though electronic and real-time journals publish research papers from all over the world, they have their own individual research evaluation and publication process. This process is not standardized across the research world, and it is not a democratic process. Furthermore, academic worth is frequently measured on where you publish rather than what you publish.

Since the problem arises from the lack of centralized evaluation and publication, the solution lies in separating both processes. Journals are essential to communication and information sharing, but a transparent and independent scrutiny of research papers and ideas is necessary. To achieve this, an immediate free, journal-independent, and transparent peer review process does make sense.

Our Goal

We plan to provide this solution in the form of a double blind, pre-publication peer review system. This system will add to the traditional peer review system by building an extra independent level of assessment to the evaluation process. We are not saying that the traditional peer review process is redundant, but by adding our independent review process, we believe that research papers can be evaluated faster and with lesser bias. In fact, publication journals can use this kind of independent review process to filter outr papers and speed up the publication process.

Of course, to provide the same degree of research evaluation, we do use the same qualified and trained peer review personnel from the relevant fields. These professionals are competent and educated researchers with doctorate-level degrees in their related fields of expertise. If you have a niche research paper, we do our best to find three top-quality researchers from the same field to evaluate your text. In case we do not have a qualified professional in our database, we recruit reviewers from outside our database to evaluate your document. Understanding the MyReposit Manuscript Score Report

There is a growing feeling in academic circles that society and science would benefit from an open and transparent process of sharing information. To achieve this, the peer review process could benefit by the addition of an independent but standardized review process. The MyReposit Manuscript Score Report is designed to be just that.

The Scorecard is the cornerstone of the double blind, independent review system and it allows reviewers to evaluate the document outside the parameters of any one specific journal. This means that the MyReposit Manuscript Score Report translates and evaluates a manuscript into measurable metrics and then provides clear feedback to authors. By these reviews, authors can create well-constructed and clearly written research papers that could pass through independent journal review procedures very quickly.

You can then outpace the competition by selecting the right journal and speeding up the publication process.

The in-house team usually triages the papers by reading through the title and abstract, and rejects papers that cover the same topic. Independent Peer Review Process

For Researchers: How Does The Process Work

Step # 1 – Set up an account with our website. Choose a method of payment and pay online.

Step # 2 – MyReposit will evaluate your paper and classify it according to MESH terms, keywords, and subject area. We then evaluate the paper for plagiarism, ethical conflicts, overlapping interest and other issues. These combined checks are referred to as a Compliance Check.

Step # 3 – Three reviewers are then selected from out database of reviewers. The paper is sent to them and they complete their blinded review of the document. Each reviewer creates a MyReposit Manuscript Score. They also create bulleted comments for the author.

Step # 4- The reviews and scores are collected and compiled into a single document called the MyReposit Manuscript Score. The report will have numerical values that indicate quality of research, presentation, interest area, value, etc. These scores are then used to calculate the Manuscript Score.

Step # 5 – Using the score, the MyReposit team then matches the score to the list of journals they have in their database. This will tell you exactly which journal will be interested in this particular research paper. We also provide you with a journal recommendation list in which we provide information about each journal, their acceptance/ rejection rates and timelines for publications.

Step # 6 – You can then log into the account and view the MyReposit Report, the MyReposit Manuscript Score Report and Compliance Check. You can also check the journal recommendation list.

Step # 7- Using our list, you can then select a journal and send your paper for publication. In case revisions are required, you can do the revisions (recommended) and then submit the paper to the right journal.

Step # 8 - When you are submitting the paper to the journal, make sure you attach the MyReposit report and submit it under ‘supplementary materials.’ In case changes were required, make sure you list these details in your cover letter.

For Reviewers: How Does the Process Work

Step # 1 – Open an account with the website. Complete the application form in as much detail as possible.

Step # 2 – If you are accepted, add in more details on the online profile page. Make sure you have set your availability, subject preferences, and other details on the dashboard. It is a good idea to also complete the online training section on how to use the scorecard and how to navigate the MyReposit website.

Step # 3 – The website database then uses an online system to match your educational qualifications and preferences to available research papers. These papers will then appear under your Reviewer Dashboard.

Step # 4 - Accept or reject papers available on your dashboard.

Step # 5 – Evaluate the paper and then complete the scorecard available on the system. These comments and scores will be collated into the MyReposit Manuscript Score Report. The report is sent to the author and it will be available to other reviewers who are working on the document.

Step # 6 – In case feedback is available; it will appear on your dashboard. You will then be compensated for your work and you can choose your payment method on the dashboard.

Understanding and Using the Manuscript Score Report

Step #1 – Read the report carefully. Understand the comments made by the reviewer. Decide if you want to make the changes requested by the reviewer based on your knowledge.

Step # 2 – Please do take the time to check the iThenticate report. Make sure you correct any possible plagiarism issues right away.

Step # 3 – You can attach a copy of the MyReposit Manuscript Score Report to your manuscript when you are submitting it to a journal. The Score Report can be attached in the PDF form or you can add the following link scorecards.rubriq.com to your unique verification code. This will allow the journal to view the report online. If you have made changes to your manuscript based on the Score Report, make sure you indicate these revisions in your cover letter.
